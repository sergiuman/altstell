# The Magic Campfire

Long, long ago, in a time when the world was very wild and very green, humans did not live in houses or towns. They lived in the forest, sleeping under the trees, wrapping themselves in big leaves when it was cold.

They were very small, and the forest was very big. There were giant cave lions with sharp teeth, and woolly mammoths that could step on a bush and flatten it. The little humans were often afraid. They ran from the lions and hid in dark caves, shivering in the cold.

Back then, humans lived in small groups, just like the forest monkeys. They spent all day looking for berries and nuts. If a human from another valley walked by, they would throw stones and yell: 

"Go away! This is our berry bush!" 

They could not work together because they did not know each other.

But one night, a bright lightning bolt hit a dry pine tree. The tree caught fire, glowing with warm, orange light. The little humans did not run away. They approached the warm light slowly, holding hands. 

They took a burning branch and carried it to a circle of stones. This was the first campfire.

The campfire changed everything. It was warm. It kept the scary lions away, because animals are afraid of fire. And for the first time, humans sat down together in a circle, looking at each other's faces.

They began to talk. Not just about berries, but about other things. They pointed to the sparks flying up into the night sky and said: 

"Look! The sparks are like glowing fireflies that live in the clouds!"

They began to make up stories. They sat close together and sang:

*“Around the fire we gather and share,*
*Stories that fly through the night air!”*

One evening, an old woman named Sola stood up by the fire. She looked at the dark forest and said, "Beyond the big river, there is a mountain shaped like a sleeping bear. I believe that on that mountain, there is a giant valley full of sweet blueberries. If we work together, we can cross the river and find it."

The humans had never seen the blueberry valley. It was just a story. But as they listened to Sola, they all pictured the sweet blue berries in their minds. They believed the story.

"Let's go!" said the young hunters.

"Let's build a boat!" said the builders.

Before the campfire, humans only worked in groups of five or ten. But because they all believed Sola's story about the blueberry valley, three different groups—nearly fifty people—decided to work together!

They gathered long branches and tied them with vines to make a giant raft. They paddled across the wide, rushing river together, singing Sola's song. And when they reached the other side, they found the mountain shaped like a sleeping bear. 

Just like in the story, the valley was covered in sweet, blue berries!

They sat around a new campfire that night, their mouths stained blue and happy. They realized that stories were like magic glue. A lion could not build a boat. A monkey could not make a plan to cross a river. But humans could, because they could share stories and believe in them together.

From that day on, humans used stories to do amazing things. They made up stories about the stars, stories about spirits, and stories about how to help each other. They built villages, planted golden corn fields, and sailed across the deep blue oceans. 

Whenever they met a stranger, they did not throw stones anymore. Instead, they asked: 

"What stories do you know?"

And they sat down to share a campfire.

Even today, when you read a book, play a game with friends, or listen to a bedtime tale, you are sitting around that same magic campfire. We are all part of one big, human family, connected by the beautiful stories we share.
