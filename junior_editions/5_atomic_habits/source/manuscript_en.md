# Tiny Steps of the Little Bear

Deep in the green woods of Eldoria lived a little brown bear named Ursu. Ursu was small, with fluffy ears, a round belly, and a very big dream. He wanted to build a giant stone castle. 

"I want a castle with a smooth stone floor, thick warm walls, and a cozy moss bed to sleep in during the long, cold winter," Ursu said.

But Ursu was only a very small bear. 

He walked to the river and tried to lift a giant stone. He pushed and pulled, but the stone would not budge. He tried another one, but it was too heavy. His arms hurt, his back hurt, and he was very tired.

Ursu sat on a log, covered his eyes with his fluffy paws, and began to cry. 

"I am too small," he sobbed. "I will never build my castle. I will have to sleep in the cold wind."

Just then, Kiri and Pip walked by. They saw Ursu crying.

"Why are you crying, little bear?" asked Kiri gently.

"I want to build a stone castle, but the stones are too heavy, and I am not strong enough," Ursu said, sniffing.

Pip jumped onto Ursu's shoulder and pointed to the ground. Near the log, a long line of tiny forest ants was marching. 

"Look!" Pip chattered. "What are the ants doing?"

Ursu looked closely. Each tiny ant was carrying a single grain of sand. They were very small, much smaller than Ursu. But they were marching to a giant sand mound next to the tree root.

"That sand castle is huge!" Ursu said in wonder. "How did they build it?"

"They did not build it in one day," Kiri explained. "Every morning, they carry one grain. Every afternoon, they carry another. They do not try to carry the whole hill at once. They just make tiny steps, every single day."

Ursu looked at his paws, then at the ants. He understood. He stood up, wiped his tears, and sang:

*“Step by step and stone by stone,*
*We build a castle of our own!”*

"I can't carry the giant stones," Ursu said. "But I *can* carry one small pebble. Just one!"

Ursu picked up a small, smooth grey pebble from the path. It was light and easy to carry. He placed it under the big fir tree. 

"That is my start," he smiled.

The next morning, as soon as Ursu woke up, he did not try to carry a mountain. He just walked to the stream, picked up one small stone, and carried it to the tree. 

Before he went to sleep, he did the same thing. One stone in the morning, one stone in the evening. It was so easy, it felt like a game.

Soon, Ursu made a rule for himself: 

*After I wash my face in the stream, I will carry my morning stone.* 

*After I eat my sweet honey dinner, I will carry my evening stone.*

Weeks passed. The leaves on the trees turned yellow and gold. Pip and Kiri sometimes came to help, bringing their own small stones. But mostly, it was Ursu. 

Every single day, without missing a turn, Ursu carried his two small stones. 

One day, the first white snowflake fell from the sky. The cold winter wind began to blow. Ursu walked to the big fir tree. 

He rubbed his eyes in surprise! 

The pile of small stones was not a pile anymore. It was a beautiful, sturdy little stone cave-castle! The walls were thick and strong, keeping out the cold wind. Inside, there was a soft bed of green moss and dry leaves.

It was perfect. It was cozy. It was his dream castle!

Ursu crawled inside, curled up into a warm brown ball, and looked out at the falling snow. He felt warm, proud, and very happy.

"I did it," he whispered, as his eyes grew heavy. "The ants were right."

*“Step by step and stone by stone,*
*We build a castle of our own!”*

And the little bear fell asleep, dreaming of the warm spring, knowing that no dream is too big if you are willing to make it out of tiny, daily steps.
